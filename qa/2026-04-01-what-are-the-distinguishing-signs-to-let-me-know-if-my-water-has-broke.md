Explanation

When your water breaks, you may feel a burst or steady leak of liquid that is not urine. Often, women describe it as a sudden gush or a continuous trickle of fluid. This fluid is usually clear and odorless, much like water. Unlike normal vaginal discharge, this wetness does not fade quickly or go away if you change your clothes. You may also notice that the fluid drips steadily, even when you are at rest. Some women feel a little wetness around their lower belly or between their legs, and if you pick up on that, it might be a sign of premature rupture of membranes. It is important to pay attention to how much fluid there is and whether it continues to leak. If you suspect that your water has broken, contact your healthcare provider right away to check on you and your baby’s well-being. They can help confirm if your membranes have ruptured and guide you on what steps to take next.
