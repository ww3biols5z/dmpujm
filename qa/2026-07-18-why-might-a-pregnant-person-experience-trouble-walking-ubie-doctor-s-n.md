Explanation

During pregnancy, the body undergoes shifts as the baby grows. Extra weight—particularly in the abdomen—moves the center of gravity forward. To maintain balance, the body adapts its movement patterns, resulting in a waddling gait where the hips sway from side to side. Research shows that this gait change helps the body stay stable when weight shifts occur. While this adaptation is helpful, it can lead to muscle strain and joint pain. The hips, lower back, and legs often become fatigued more quickly as they work harder to support the redistributed weight. Pregnant individuals should receive guidance on safe movements and may benefit from exercises or physical therapy to reduce discomfort and improve walking stability.
