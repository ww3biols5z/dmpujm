Explanation

Colon cancer, also known as colorectal cancer, can sometimes lead to the formation of a mass or lump in the abdomen, especially in advanced stages when the tumor increases in size or if there is spread (metastasis) to surrounding tissues. However, it is important to note that many lumps in the abdomen are due to benign conditions, such as cysts, lipomas (non-cancerous fatty tumors), or hernias. In some reported cases, colon cancers have even masqueraded as other abdominal conditions, making diagnosis more challenging. Typically, colon cancer is also associated with other symptoms such as changes in bowel habits, unexplained weight loss, or blood in the stool. If you notice a new lump in your abdomen along with any concerning symptoms, it is important to have it evaluated by a healthcare provider. They may recommend imaging studies, colonoscopy, and possibly a biopsy to determine whether the lump is related to colon cancer or due to another cause.
