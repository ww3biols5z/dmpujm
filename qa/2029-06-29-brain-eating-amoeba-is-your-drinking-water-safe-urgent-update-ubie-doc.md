Brain-eating amoeba (Naegleria fowleri) cannot infect you through drinking water. Infection only occurs when contaminated warm freshwater enters the body through the nose, allowing the amoeba to travel to the brain. While rare, the resulting infection (primary amebic meningoencephalitis) is often fatal, so prevention matters.

Symptoms like severe headache, fever, nausea, stiff neck, or confusion after freshwater exposure should never be ignored — but these signs can also point to many other, more common conditions. Explanation

The brain-eating amoeba, known as Naegleria fowleri, is not a risk when drinking water. However, it can cause serious illness if it enters the body through the nose. This usually happens when people swim or dive in warm, fresh water like lakes and rivers.

Key Points:

[Your Drinking Water Safe](https://glycometrix.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/1603078422.html)

Naegleria fowleri is a tiny organism found in warm freshwater.

It becomes dangerous when it enters the body through the nose, not by drinking.

Activities like swimming, diving, or using neti pots with contaminated water can pose a risk.

If the amoeba enters the nose, it can travel to the brain and cause a serious infection called primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM). This condition is very rare but often fatal. If you're experiencing concerning symptoms after potential exposure to contaminated water, use our free AI symptom checker to help understand what might be happening. To reduce risk, avoid getting water up your nose when swimming and use distilled or boiled water for nasal rinses.
