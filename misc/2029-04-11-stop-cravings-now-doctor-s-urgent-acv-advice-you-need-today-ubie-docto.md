Explanation

Sugar cravings can derail even the most committed healthy-eating plans. You're not alone if you reach for a candy bar in the afternoon or struggle to resist dessert. Fortunately, apple cider vinegar (ACV) has been studied for its potential to curb these urges. Below, we explain how to use apple cider vinegar for sugar cravings safely and effectively, backed by credible research and clinical insights.

Why We Get Sugar Cravings

Before diving into apple cider vinegar for sugar cravings, it helps to understand what drives these urges:

Blood sugar dips. A rapid drop in blood glucose after a high-carb meal signals your brain to seek quick energy.

Habit and environment. Certain times of day or situations (stress, boredom) become triggers.

Hormonal shifts. Fluctuations in insulin, cortisol, and ghrelin (the "hunger hormone") affect your appetite.

Nutrient deficiencies. Sometimes a craving signals that you need more protein, fiber or healthy fats.

Emotional factors. Stress, fatigue and emotional distress often lead us to seek comfort foods.

How Apple Cider Vinegar Works

Several small clinical trials suggest that ACV can help stabilize blood sugar and reduce appetite, making it a useful tool for sugar-craving curb:

Slows gastric emptying. Acetic acid—the active component in ACV—can delay how quickly food leaves your stomach, helping you feel full longer.¹

Improves insulin sensitivity. Research published in Diabetes Care found that taking vinegar with a high-carb meal improved insulin response by 19–34%.²

Blunts blood sugar spikes. By reducing post-meal glucose surges, ACV may lessen the cycle of highs and lows that fuel cravings.

While these effects are modest, combining ACV with a balanced diet, regular exercise and good sleep can yield noticeable improvements.

Using Apple Cider Vinegar for Sugar Cravings

If you decide to try apple cider vinegar for sugar cravings, follow these guidelines:

Look for "with the mother" on the label (sediment containing enzymes and friendly bacteria).

Begin with 1 teaspoon (5 ml) diluted in 8 ounces of water once per day.

Move up to 1–2 tablespoons (15–30 ml) per day, split between meals.

Drink ACV 10–15 minutes before your main carbohydrate-containing meals to blunt blood sugar spikes.

Add a squeeze of lemon or a dash of cinnamon to reduce bitterness.

Stir in a little honey or stevia if needed, but keep sweeteners to a minimum.

Safety and Precautions

Apple cider vinegar is generally safe for most people when used in moderation. However, be aware of:

Tooth enamel erosion. Always dilute ACV and consider drinking through a straw. Rinse your mouth afterward with plain water.

Throat irritation. If you feel a burning sensation, dilute more heavily or reduce frequency.

Gastrointestinal discomfort. Some people experience gas or nausea; reduce dose or stop if severe.

Drug interactions. ACV can interact with diuretics, insulin or digoxin. If you're on medication, discuss with your doctor first.

Never exceed 2 tablespoons (30 ml) per day without medical supervision.

Lifestyle Strategies to Complement ACV

Relying solely on apple cider vinegar for sugar cravings won't solve the underlying habits. Combine ACV with these proven tactics:

Balance your meals. Aim for a mix of lean protein, fiber-rich carbohydrates and healthy fats at every meal to stabilize blood sugar.

Stay hydrated. Sometimes thirst masquerades as hunger. Drink water consistently throughout the day.

Prioritize sleep. Poor sleep disrupts hunger hormones, leading to stronger cravings. Aim for 7–9 hours nightly.

When to Seek Professional Help

Persistent, intense cravings may signal a deeper issue, such as insulin resistance, hormonal imbalance or an eating disorder pattern. If you experience any of the following, you should speak to a healthcare professional:

Frequent dizziness, sweating or confusion between meals

Uncontrolled weight gain or loss

Feelings of guilt or distress around eating

Binge eating episodes

Final Thoughts

Apple cider vinegar can be a valuable adjunct to your toolkit for reducing sugar cravings. By slowing gastric emptying, improving insulin sensitivity and helping you feel full, ACV—when used properly—may help you regain control over your diet. That said, it's not a magic bullet. Pair it with a balanced diet, consistent exercise, good sleep and stress management for the best results.

If you're taking medications or have a chronic health condition, always check with your doctor before starting any new supplement or dietary regimen.
