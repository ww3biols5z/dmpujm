Selvedge and a nice button-up might seem like a quick fix to looking polished, but it’s the small details that really make the look. Sure, you make sure to press your pants and keep your shoes free of scuffs, but when was the last time you checked your collar before you left the house?

“The collar of a shirt is meant to frame the face and highlight the facial structure,” says celebrity stylist Samantha Brown. One that’s limp and wrinkled isn’t framing anything. Often overlooked, a wonky collar turns your sleek, buttoned-up look into a sloppy mess. But there are a few things you can do about it.

Collar Variety

Your best bet is starting with a solid foundation. Choose a shirt with a decent placket (the line of fabric that holds your buttons down the front of the shirt), which acts as a backbone and will provide stability to the collar. Your collar type also makes a difference. “Spread collars and cutaway collars tend to be lighter and retain their shape,” Brown says, “while heavier, pointed collars can sag.” Both spread and cutaway collars have more space between the points; the flatter and more relaxed shape is easier to maintain. Classic collars, on the other hand, need work to keep them stiffened and straight.

Collar Stays

Collar stays are a good option when you’re pressed for time because they require significantly less prep work than ironing—simply slide the stays into your collar. They come in a variety of materials, from plastic to wood to metal. Most shirts have openings for traditional collar stays, but there are magnetic and adhesive versions that are designed to work without a pocket in the collar. Whichever variety you choose, just remember to take them out before putting your shirt in the wash.

“Spray starch and ironing are a great way to give a collar some structure,” Brown says. To keep your collar from flattening throughout the day, spray a layer of starch on the collar and let it sit for a few minutes before ironing. Ironing your collar into place is best, but if you only have a steamer on hand, that will also work.

“Keep in mind that consistent starching will wear down the fabric of the shirt,” Brown explains, “so it’s not recommended for consistent care.” While yellowing around the collar is a tell-tale sign of wear, dry cleaning can help prolong the life of your shirt.

Button Down Collars

If you can’t be bothered with collar stays or ironing, your best bet is investing in button down collar shirts. Many come with buttons already attached to the collar to keep everything in place. It doesn’t get easier than that.
