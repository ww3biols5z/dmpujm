Sciatica vs. Diabetic Neuropathy: How to Tell the Difference

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Sciatica vs. diabetic neuropathy: what's the difference? Sciatica causes sudden, sharp, one-sided pain radiating from the low back down to the foot, often worsening with movement and easing with rest. Diabetic neuropathy, by contrast, develops gradually as burning or tingling in both feet, typically worsens at night, and is linked to long-term high blood sugar.

Key differences include onset (sudden vs. gradual), pain distribution (one-sided vs. both feet), triggers, and underlying medical history. Each condition has distinct risk factors, symptom patterns, and diagnostic tests, and treatment approaches vary significantly.

Because these conditions can overlap or mimic other nerve issues, self-diagnosing is risky and may delay care. Explanation

Experiencing a sharp, shooting pain from hip to foot can be alarming. Two common causes are sciatica and diabetic neuropathy. Both can cause discomfort along the leg, but knowing which one you have is essential for proper treatment. This guide will help you understand the key differences, recognize warning signs, and decide when to seek medical advice.

What Is Sciatica?

Sciatica is pain resulting from irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve, which runs from your lower back down each leg.

Causes

Herniated or bulging spinal discs

Spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)

Piriformis syndrome (muscle pressing on the nerve)

Injury or repetitive stress

Typical Symptoms

Sharp, shooting pain from hip to foot, often on one side

Pain worsens when sitting, coughing, or sneezing

Numbness or tingling in the leg or foot

What Is Diabetic Neuropathy?

Diabetic neuropathy is nerve damage caused by chronically high blood sugar levels in people with diabetes. It primarily affects sensory and autonomic nerves.

Poorly controlled blood sugar over months or years

Metabolic factors that damage nerve fibers

How to Tell Which You Have

While only a healthcare provider can confirm a diagnosis, these clues can guide you:

Location of Pain

Sciatica: Unilateral (one side) from low back down to foot

Neuropathy: Symmetrical (both feet), often starts in toes/soles

Type of Sensation

Sciatica: Electric shocks, sharp shooting pain from hip to foot

Neuropathy: Burning, tingling, numbness, like "pins and needles"

Timing

Sciatica: Pain spikes with certain movements, improves with rest

Neuropathy: Constant or night-worseing pain, less tied to activity

Medical History

Sciatica: Recent back injury, heavy lifting, spinal arthritis

Neuropathy: Known diabetes, elevated blood sugar readings

Physical Exam Findings

Sciatica: Positive straight-leg raise test, decreased spinal mobility

Neuropathy: Reduced vibration or temperature sense in feet, decreased ankle reflexes

Treatment Approaches

Sciatica

Physical therapy focusing on stretching and strengthening

NSAIDs or muscle relaxants for pain relief

Epidural steroid injections for severe cases

Surgery (e.g., discectomy) if conservative treatments fail and nerve function worsens

Diabetic Neuropathy

Blood sugar control through diet, exercise, and medications

Pain management with anticonvulsants (gabapentin), antidepressants, or topical agents

Foot care: daily inspection, proper footwear

Lifestyle changes: quitting smoking, limiting alcohol

Self-Care and Prevention

Maintain a healthy weight to reduce stress on your spine and nerves

Practice good posture and safe lifting techniques

Control blood sugar with a balanced diet and regular exercise

Inspect feet daily if you have diabetes, looking for cuts or blisters

Stretch gently before and after activities

Don't Wait—Speak to a Doctor

Distinguishing between sciatica and diabetic neuropathy is not always straightforward. Persistent or worsening symptoms require professional evaluation. If you experience severe pain, sudden weakness, or signs of infection, seek medical attention right away. Always speak to a doctor about any health concern that could be life-threatening or serious.

By recognizing the differences in pain patterns, risk factors, and diagnostic findings, you can take the right steps toward relief. Whether it's addressing a herniated disc or managing blood sugar levels, getting the correct diagnosis is your first move toward feeling better.
