Right-sided chest pain is less commonly linked to serious heart conditions than left-sided chest pain, but it should never be ignored. Possible causes include muscle strain, lung issues, digestive problems, or, in some cases, cardiovascular concerns. Because symptoms can overlap across many conditions, a proper evaluation by a healthcare provider is essential to identify the underlying cause and determine the right treatment.

When you feel pain on the right side of your chest, it often comes from causes that are not heart-related. Studies have shown that heart problems more commonly cause pain on the left side, so right side pain is more likely due to things like muscle strain, inflammation of the chest wall, or lung issues. For instance, conditions like pleuritic chest pain (pain that happens when the lining of the lung gets angry) or even hyperventilation syndrome (where breathing too fast causes discomfort) can lead to pain on the right side. There's also a condition called Mondor's disease that affects the chest wall, and it can cause pain on the right side too. However, even though right side chest pain is less likely to be from a heart attack, any chest pain that comes on suddenly or is severe should be taken seriously.
