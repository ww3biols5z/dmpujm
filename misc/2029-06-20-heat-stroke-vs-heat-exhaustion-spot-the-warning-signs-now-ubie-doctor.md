Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are both heat-related illnesses, but they differ in severity. Heat exhaustion is the milder form, marked by heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, and cool, clammy skin. Heat stroke is a medical emergency, characterized by a body temperature above 103°F, confusion, rapid pulse, and often hot, dry skin with little or no sweating.

Recognizing the difference matters—heat stroke can cause organ damage or death within minutes if untreated. If you're unsure which condition you or a loved one may be experiencing, don't guess. When you have heat exhaustion, you might feel very tired, have a headache, feel dizzy, and your body sweats a lot. Your body temperature is usually only a little high. On the other hand, heat stroke is much more dangerous. With heat stroke, your body temperature is very high, and you may feel confused, have trouble speaking, or even lose consciousness. Here are some ways to tell them apart:

In Heat Exhaustion, you will usually notice:

Heavy sweating

Weakness and dizziness

A moderately raised body temperature

In Heat Stroke, you may see:

Very high body temperature
