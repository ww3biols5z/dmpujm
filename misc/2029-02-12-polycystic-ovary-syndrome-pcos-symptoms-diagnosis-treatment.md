# Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) - Symptoms, Diagnosis, & Treatment

13 Jan Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) – Symptoms, Diagnosis, & Treatment

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a complex hormonal issue that occurs during the reproductive phase of life. Identifying its associated symptoms and signs is paramount toward the successful treatment of this challenging condition.

What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects women that are of reproductive age. If you have been experiencing irregular periods, unexplained weight changes, or other concerning symptoms, it is possible that PCOS could be the cause. Understanding what PCOS is can help empower you to take the necessary proactive steps toward managing your health and dealing with these issues.

PCOS occurs when your ovaries produce an abnormal amount of androgens. These are male sex hormones that are present in all women in small amounts. This hormonal imbalance can lead to the development of small fluid-filled sacs called cysts on the ovaries. However, it is important to note that not everyone with PCOS will have cysts, and the presence of cysts alone does not confirm that you have PCOS.

This condition can have many different effects on your health including impacting your menstrual cycle, fertility, insulin production, and even your appearance.

PCOS Symptoms

If you believe that you may be dealing with PCOS, you might notice one or several of the following signs:

Weight Fluctuations

Weight gain, especially around the abdomen, is a very common symptom of PCOS. You may find it challenging to lose weight despite eating a healthy diet and exercising regularly. This is often because of insulin resistance, which can make it harder for your body to use insulin properly, leading to an increase in weight gain. On the other hand, some women may also experience an unexplained weight loss.

PCOS and Fertility

If you are trying to conceive and are facing difficulties, PCOS could be a contributing factor. These hormonal imbalances can interfere with ovulation, making it harder to become pregnant. Irregular menstrual cycles mean that ovulation does not occur regularly, which reduces your chances of conception. However, many women with PCOS can and do become pregnant with the right treatment and proper support.

PCOS Fatigue

Feeling unusually tired or experiencing ongoing fatigue can be another symptom. This might be related to hormonal imbalances, insulin resistance, or the sleep disturbances that often come along with PCOS. If you find yourself constantly low on energy, it’s important to discuss this with your healthcare provider.

PCOS Regular Period

Irregular periods are one of the most recognized signs of PCOS. You might have fewer than nine periods a year, have periods that occur more than 35 days apart, or have abnormally heavy periods. This irregularity is caused by the hormonal imbalance that is affecting ovulation.

PCOS in Teens

PCOS can also affect teenagers. PCOS may possibly be the cause for irregular periods, severe acne, or excessive hair growth experienced by teens. Early diagnosis and management are important in order to prevent long-term complications. If you are concerned about any of the symptoms that you are experiencing, talking to a healthcare provider can help you better understand what’s happening with your body.

PCOS Diagnosis and Testing

Getting a proper diagnosis is the first step toward properly managing PCOS. Your healthcare provider will likely start with a detailed medical history and physical examination. They might ask about your menstrual cycle, weight changes, skin issues, and your family health history.

PCOS Ultrasound

An ultrasound is an essential diagnostic tool for PCOS that gives your doctor the ability to examine your ovaries and check for the presence of cysts. During a pelvic ultrasound, a wand-like device is placed in the vagina (transvaginal ultrasound) or over the abdomen. These images can show if your ovaries are enlarged or if they have multiple small cysts, which are characteristic signs of PCOS.

Blood tests may also be conducted in order to measure hormone levels, including androgens, estrogen, and progesterone. Testing for insulin resistance and lipid levels can provide additional information for a complete diagnosis.

PCOS Treatment

Managing PCOS usually requires a specialized approach that is specifically tailored to your unique combination of symptoms and health goals. Treatments can help regulate your menstrual cycle, reduce any symptoms, and improve your fertility if desired.

During Pregnancy

If you’re pregnant or are planning to become pregnant, dealing with PCOS is very important to help better ensure a healthy pregnancy. PCOS can increase the risk of complications like gestational diabetes, high blood pressure, or premature birth.

Reversing PCOS

While there is no cure for PCOS, its symptoms can often be managed and, in some cases, can be significantly reduced. Lifestyle changes such as eating a balanced diet, getting regular exercise, and staying within a healthy weight range can help improve any hormonal imbalances and insulin sensitivity.

PCOS and Endometriosis

PCOS and endometriosis are two different conditions, but some women may experience both. Endometriosis involves the growth of uterine tissue outside the uterus, leading to pain and often fertility issues. If you have symptoms of both conditions, your healthcare provider can develop a personalized treatment plan that addresses both at the same time.

Berberine and PCOS

Berberine is a natural supplement that has gained a great deal of attention for its potential benefits in helping to manage PCOS symptoms. It may help to improve insulin resistance and reduce androgen levels. However, it’s important to talk with your doctor before starting any new supplements in order to ensure it’s both safe and appropriate for you.

Progesterone for PCOS

Progesterone therapy may be prescribed to help regulate menstrual cycles and reduce the risk of endometrial hyperplasia, which can sometimes occur due to prolonged periods of time without ovulation. Taking progesterone can bring on a period and help balance out your hormones.

Letrozole for PCOS

Letrozole is a medication that is commonly used to bring on ovulation in women with PCOS who are trying to conceive. It helps to stimulate the ovaries and encourage them to release eggs. Discussing this option with your healthcare provider can help determine if it’s suitable for your fertility goals.

Hysterectomy for PCOS

A hysterectomy is the surgical removal of the uterus, and this is not a common treatment for PCOS. Since PCOS is primarily related to ovarian function and hormonal imbalances, removing the uterus will not properly address the underlying cause. This procedure is usually considered for other medical conditions and is not a standard treatment for PCOS.

Adrenal PCOS

Adrenal PCOS refers to a form of the condition where the adrenal glands produce excess androgens. Managing this type of PCOS means directly addressing adrenal function. This may include stress management techniques, because stress can impact the cortisol levels which are produced by the adrenal glands.

As you approach menopause, your PCOS symptoms may change. Some women find that menstrual irregularities begin to improve, but issues like insulin resistance and cardiovascular risks can still pose a threat. Continuing to monitor your health and manage your symptoms remains just as important during this stage of life.

Hypothyroidism and PCOS

Hypothyroidism is a condition where the thyroid gland doesn’t produce enough hormones. This can sometimes occur alongside PCOS. Symptoms like fatigue, weight gain, and menstrual irregularities can overlap. Testing thyroid function is important because treating hypothyroidism can help alleviate some of the PCOS symptoms.

Cortisol and PCOS

Cortisol is also known as the stress hormone and it can have an influence on PCOS symptoms. High cortisol levels that are caused from chronic stress may make any hormonal imbalances even worse. Incorporating stress relief techniques like mindfulness, meditation, or yoga can be a great way to naturally help manage PCOS symptoms.

Zinc for PCOS

Zinc is an essential mineral that plays a role in both hormone regulation and immune function. Some studies suggest that zinc supplementation may help reduce some of the PCOS symptoms like acne and hirsutism.

IVF for PCOS

In vitro fertilization (IVF) may be considered if other fertility treatments haven’t been successful. IVF is the process of retrieving eggs and fertilizing them with sperm in a lab, then transferring the embryo to the uterus.

PCOS Self Care

Taking an active role in managing PCOS through self-care can make a significant difference in your symptoms and overall well-being. Adopting a balanced diet that is rich in whole foods can help manage insulin resistance and promote a healthier hormonal balance. Focus on incorporating plenty of fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains. It can also be helpful to limit consumption of items like processed foods, sugars, and unhealthy fats.

Regular physical activity can also help improve insulin sensitivity, support weight management, and even boost your mood. You should strive for a combination of cardiovascular exercises, like walking or swimming, and strength training to help give your overall health a boost.

Since insulin resistance is common in PCOS, managing your blood sugar levels is also important. Besides diet and exercise, your healthcare provider might recommend certain medications like metformin to help improve your insulin sensitivity.

When to Consult with a PCOS Specialist

If you are struggling to manage your symptoms or if you have concerns about your fertility, it might be the right time to consult with a PCOS specialist or endocrinologist. They can provide specialized care, offer advanced treatment options, and create a plan that is customized to your specific needs. Early intervention can help prevent long-term complications and improve your quality of life.

Conclusion

Understanding polycystic ovary syndrome and how it can impact your health is the first step toward managing it properly. By understanding and recognizing the symptoms, undergoing testing and getting a proper diagnosis, and looking into all of the treatment options that are available, you can take control of your health and well-being. Remember, you’re not alone, and support is available. With the right care and a few easy lifestyle adjustments, many women with PCOS can lead healthy, fulfilling lives.
