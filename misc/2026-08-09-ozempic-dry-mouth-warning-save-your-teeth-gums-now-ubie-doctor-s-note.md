Doctor's Note: Why Is Your Mouth So Dry on Ozempic? Protecting Your Gums & Teeth

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Ozempic (semaglutide) can cause dry mouth because it slows stomach emptying, affects local nerves that regulate saliva, and raises your risk of dehydration. Reduced saliva flow increases the likelihood of cavities, gum disease, bad breath, and oral infections like thrush. To protect your teeth and gums, drink water consistently throughout the day, chew sugar-free gum or suck on xylitol lozenges, use over-the-counter saliva substitutes, brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, and maintain regular dental checkups.

Because dry mouth can also signal dehydration, medication interactions, or unrelated conditions, it's worth investigating your specific symptoms rather than guessing. If you're taking Ozempic (semaglutide) to manage blood sugar or support weight loss, you may notice one unexpected side effect: dry mouth. What Is Ozempic?

Ozempic is a once-weekly injectable medication used to:

Improve blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes

Promote weight loss by reducing appetite

It belongs to the GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonist class. By mimicking a gut hormone, it slows gastric emptying and enhances insulin release when blood sugar is high.

Why Does Ozempic Cause Dry Mouth?

Dry mouth (xerostomia) occurs when saliva production drops below normal. Here's why Ozempic may trigger this:

Reduced Saliva Stimulus: Slower stomach emptying may trick your body into thinking less digestion is needed, indirectly diminishing saliva production.

Local Nerve Effects: GLP-1 receptors exist in nerves that help regulate saliva glands. When Ozempic activates these receptors, saliva output can decline.

Dehydration Risk: Nausea, vomiting or reduced fluid intake—common early on—can contribute to dehydration and dry mouth.

Dry mouth may feel like:

Sticky, parched feeling in your mouth

Difficulty speaking, chewing or swallowing

Cracked lips or corners of the mouth

When saliva is low, your teeth and gums lose a vital defense mechanism. Saliva:

Neutralizes acids from food and bacteria

Washes away food particles and sugars

Provides calcium and phosphate to strengthen teeth

How Dry Mouth Increases Risk for Oral Problems

Without enough saliva, you're more vulnerable to:

Tooth Decay (Cavities): Acids build up and bacteria flourish.

Gum Disease (Gingivitis/Periodontitis): Plaque accumulates at the gum line, leading to inflammation and infection.

Oral Thrush: Fungal overgrowth in a dry environment.

Bad Breath (Halitosis): Bacteria produce foul-smelling compounds.

Mouth Sores and Cracks: Skin around the mouth can split or ulcerate.

Together, these conditions underscore why monitoring "Ozempic and teeth issues" is essential for your overall well-being.

Practical Tips to Protect Your Gums & Teeth

Hydrate Strategically

Sip water throughout the day rather than gulping large amounts at once.

Carry a reusable water bottle as a reminder.

Try ice chips or sugar-free ice pops during the day.

Stimulate Saliva Flow

Chew sugar-free gum or suck on sugar-free hard candies (look for xylitol, which also fights cavities).

Consider an over-the-counter saliva substitute or mouth moisturizer gel.

Optimize Oral Hygiene

Brush twice daily with a fluoride toothpaste.

Use a soft-bristled brush to avoid irritating gums.

Floss once daily to remove plaque where your brush can't reach.

Rinse with an alcohol-free, fluoride mouthwash.

Choose Tooth-Friendly Foods

Limit sugary, sticky, or acidic snacks and drinks.

Opt for crunchy fruits and vegetables (e.g., apples, celery) that help clean teeth naturally.

Include dairy or calcium-fortified alternatives to strengthen enamel.

Regular Dental Visits

Schedule checkups every 6 months or as recommended by your dentist.

Let your dental team know you're on Ozempic so they can monitor for signs of dry mouth and decay.

Monitor Medication Timing

If you take other medications that cause dry mouth (e.g., antihistamines, antidepressants), ask your doctor if dosing adjustments are possible.

When to Seek Professional Help

Pay attention to warning signs that warrant a prompt call or visit:

Persistent dry mouth lasting more than two weeks

New or worsening tooth sensitivity, pain, or cavities

Bleeding, swollen or receding gums

White patches (possible thrush) or red, inflamed areas in your mouth
