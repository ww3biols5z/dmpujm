Can thin people also have sleep apnea?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, thin people can have sleep apnea. While excess weight is a well-known risk factor, sleep apnea also affects individuals with a normal or low body mass index (BMI).

Common causes in thin people include:

Anatomical factors: A narrow airway, enlarged tonsils, a recessed jaw, or a deviated septum can obstruct breathing during sleep.

Age and muscle tone: Throat muscles naturally relax with age, increasing airway collapse risk.

Genetics: Family history of sleep apnea raises risk regardless of weight.

Central sleep apnea: A neurological form unrelated to body size, where the brain fails to signal breathing muscles properly.

Symptoms like loud snoring, gasping during sleep, morning headaches, and daytime fatigue should never be dismissed based on body type alone. Untreated sleep apnea can lead to serious complications, including high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke.

Explanation

Sleep apnea is a condition where breathing stops and starts during sleep. While it is common in people who are overweight, thin people can have it too.

What the Studies Say:

Non-Obese Sleep Apnea: One study shows that sleep apnea can happen in people who are not obese. This means that even if someone is thin, they might still have sleep apnea.

Common in Thin People: Another study found that sleep apnea without obesity is more common than people think. This means thin people can have sleep apnea just like others.

Normal Weight Patients: A third study talks about how people with normal weight can also have sleep apnea. This shows that weight is not the only reason for sleep apnea.

In summary, while being overweight is a risk factor for sleep apnea, thin individuals can also have the condition. It is important for everyone, regardless of weight, to be aware of the symptoms of sleep apnea, such as loud snoring or feeling very tired during the day. If you're experiencing any of these symptoms, you can check your symptoms online or seek medical advice from a healthcare professional.
