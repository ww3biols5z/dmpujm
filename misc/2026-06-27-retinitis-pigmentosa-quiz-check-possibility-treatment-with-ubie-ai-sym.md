What is Retinitis pigmentosa?

An inherited degenerative disease affecting the retina, the nerve layer lining the back of the eye. It causes loss of night and side vision.

Typical Symptoms of Retinitis pigmentosa

Changes in color perception

Partial loss of vision

Sensitivity to bright light

Vision problems

Difficulty seeing in the dark

Difficulty seeing on one side

Difficulty seeing due to poor color or light contrast

Difficulty seeing which affects daily life e.g. driving, climbing stairs

Diagnostic Questions for Retinitis pigmentosa

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Have you experienced any changes in your color perception?

Have you experienced partial loss of vision?

Are you sensitive to bright lights?

Are you experiencing any vision problems?

Do you experience difficulty seeing in the dark?

Treatment of Retinitis pigmentosa

There is no specific treatment for retinitis pigmentosa. Protecting the retina with UV sunglasses may help delay the onset of symptoms. In cases of very advanced disease and severe vision loss, a retinal implant may be helpful.
