What Are NAD+ Injections?

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, or NAD+, is a naturally occurring coenzyme found in every cell of your body. It plays a critical role in cellular metabolism, helping convert the food you eat into usable energy. Beyond energy production, NAD+ is also involved in DNA repair, mitochondrial function, and maintaining overall cellular health. (1)

As men age, NAD+ levels naturally decline. This drop has been associated with reduced energy, slower recovery, and changes in metabolic function. That connection has led to increased interest in NAD+ supplementation as a way to support wellness and longevity.

NAD+ injections deliver this coenzyme directly into the body, bypassing the digestive system. This method may allow for faster absorption and more immediate availability at the cellular level compared to oral supplements. (2) Depending on the treatment plan, injections may be administered on a scheduled basis to help maintain consistent levels.

Possible Side Effects of NAD+ Injections

Like any treatment, NAD+ injections can come with side effects for some men. The good news is that most reported reactions are mild and temporary. Still, understanding what you might experience helps you prepare and respond appropriately.

Common side effects of using NAD+ injections may include: (3)

Temporary discomfort at the injection site, such as redness, swelling, or soreness

Mild nausea, especially if the injection is administered quickly

Flushing or a warm sensation in the body

Mild to moderate headaches

Pressure or tightness in the chest

Temporary fatigue or lightheadedness during or shortly after treatment

These effects are typically short-lived and resolve on their own without intervention. Many men find that side effects lessen over time as their body becomes more accustomed to the therapy.

Some people may also notice subtle changes in how they feel during the first few sessions; for example, a brief period of fatigue may be followed by improved energy levels later in the day. These shifts are often part of the body adjusting to increased NAD+ availability.

While rare, more pronounced symptoms should always be discussed with a clinician. This includes persistent nausea, dizziness that does not resolve, or any reaction that feels out of the ordinary.

Are NAD+ Injections Safe?
