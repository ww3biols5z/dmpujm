Internal Medicine

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological sleep disorder that causes excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) and, in some cases, sudden muscle weakness triggered by strong emotions, known as cataplexy. Other common symptoms include sleep paralysis, hallucinations when falling asleep or waking, and disrupted nighttime sleep. Diagnosis typically involves a detailed medical history, an overnight polysomnogram (sleep study), and a Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT) to measure how quickly you fall asleep during the day.

Because narcolepsy shares symptoms with many other conditions, identifying the cause of your sleepiness early can make a meaningful difference in treatment and quality of life. Explanation

Narcolepsy is a condition where people feel very sleepy during the day, even if they sleep well at night. This can lead to falling asleep suddenly, even in the middle of activities. Another main symptom is cataplexy, which is a sudden loss of muscle strength, often triggered by strong emotions like laughter or surprise.

Diagnosis

To diagnose narcolepsy, doctors often use two sleep studies:

Polysomnography (PSG): This overnight test measures sleep cycles and checks for other sleep disorders.

Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT): This test checks how quickly a person falls asleep in a quiet environment during the day.

Doctors also look at the person's medical history and symptoms to make a diagnosis.
